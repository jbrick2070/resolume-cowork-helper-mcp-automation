# Dew Line - deck sheets

A spider builds an early-warning station out of silk, takes her own scaffold down before dawn, and casts her shadow on every star the net catches.


## Deck 1 - Orb Plate 1889

An orb web built from nothing between two hedge stems before sunrise, drawn the way Henry McCook engraved it in 1889 and stamped the way a 1962 drawing office would stamp it: bone thread on slate, every radius numbered, a title block bottom-left that says DO NOT SCALE. The light is the grey half-hour before the sun finds the dew. The wonder is procedural: one animal, one line, forty minutes, a structure whose scaffold she eats as she lays the capture spiral and whose finished work she eats at dawn so the protein can be spun again. Register: Victorian plate engraving, drafting-office revision letters, specimen not taken.

## Deck 2 - Sit and Wait

A funnel web in long grass at three in the morning, seen from inside the funnel: the silk sheet is a drumskin and the spider is downstairs in the retreat, reading it through slits in her legs. A sodium streetlamp on a timer is the only light from above; nothing moves until something lands, and then bearing, amplitude, decision, rush. The dread is that stillness is the trap and she can wait twelve days; the wonder is that she tells wind from rain from prey by frequency alone. Register: 1970s nocturnal field biology, a seismograph pen on a paper drum, the darkest deck of the five.

## Deck 4 - Goose Summer

Inside the egg sac is the quietest room in the world: three hundred eggs in wound silk, lit through from outside like a lamp through linen, with the mother on the outside not eating and not leaving, and in some species the first meal. Then the moult, where a spider leaves an exact ghost of herself and walks out soft; then the spiderlings on tiptoe at the top of a fence post, abdomens to the wind, throwing a line into the sky's electric field and letting go. Darwin found the Beagle's rigging coated in their silk sixty miles from land, and Middle English called the stuff gossamer, goose summer. The light is milk and amber on the only light ground in the instrument, 16mm natural history circa 1979, microscope-close and heavy with grain; the register is a naturalist's tenderness in lower case, and this is the deck you switch to when the room needs to breathe.

## Deck 5 - Dew Line Station

A Cold War early-warning radar station whose antenna sweep leaves an orb web as its trace: the DEW Line of 1957 and dew on a web at dawn are the same picture, a net strung across the dark that catches light so you can see what is coming. The dome is a telescope too, and every telescope already contains a spider: the four vanes that hold the secondary mirror cast a cross on every bright star, and surveyors once strung their reticles with a single strand of silk. Louise Bourgeois' Maman stands over the dance floor, nine metres of steel seen from underneath with marble eggs held in the sac; Arabella spun in Skylab in 1973; CP1919 ticks every 1.337 seconds and was not a signal, it was a clock. The light is P7 phosphor, signal white and tape-reel amber on blue-black, 1981 regional continuity with no warmth at all, and the deck ends in a legitimate black, a test card, and a stop.
