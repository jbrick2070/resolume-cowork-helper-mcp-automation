# Dead Air - deck sheets

Five rooms where someone is listening to the dark, and the dark, occasionally, replies.


## Deck 1 - Hydrogen Line

The night shift at a listening post, 1967 to 1995 but always three in the morning: a hut, a chart recorder drawing ninety-six feet of paper a day, a P1 phosphor tube, and a sky that is pure hiss at 2.725 kelvin until it is not. The light is sensor-bias grey, buff paper and one pen lamp; chart-paper white is the only thing allowed to glow, and hydrogen-alpha red arrives once, for seventy-two seconds. The wonder is clerical: a quarter-inch of scruff that repeats every 1.337 seconds, WOW! in a margin, three thousand galaxies developing on a plate; the dread is that the pulse is too regular to be natural. Register: teletype telemetry in OCR A Extended, bottom-left and never centred, right ascension and declination, LISTENING, NOT SENDING.

## Deck 2 - Loss Of Signal

Mission Control's voice loop at the moment the spacecraft goes behind the Moon: LOS, forty-five minutes of dead air, and the whistling the Apollo 10 crew heard on the far side and nearly did not report. The room is console grey, P39 phosphor green and one amber tally lamp; everything else is the dark the carrier left behind, and in this room the scope is the picture, full width along the lower third. The dread is that the loop keeps counting into silence and the voice that eventually answers may not be on the roster; the wonder is AOS, the sunrise that comes back on schedule as one long white flash. Period register is 1969 Houston in Consolas: teletype paper, capcom cadence, left-justified, hiss at 2.2 kHz.
