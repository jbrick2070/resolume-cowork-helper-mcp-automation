# Videonics MX-1: sourced facts

Gathered 2026-10-23 for the MX-1 deck. Every caption on that deck comes from a line here.

Sources: [M] Videonics MX-1 Instruction Manual MANL-0521-02, 1994, 93 pp https://www.brianhilmers.com/gear/manuals/videonics-mx1-ntsc-digital-video-mixer.pdf (mirror https://www.manualslib.com/manual/239742/Videonics-Mx-1.html); "p." is the printed page · [VM] Videomaker https://www.videomaker.com/article/c5/3497-mx-1-video-mixer-is-a-product-for-the-mass-audience/ · [FE] https://www.videomaker.com/videonews/2004/08/focus-enhancements-releases-new-video-mixers/ · [SEC] Videonics 10-K/A https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/932113/0001013523-98-000004.txt · [WP] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VJing · dealer ads: Google Books snippets, named inline

## Identity
- Videonics, Inc., 1370 Dell Avenue, Campbell, CA 95008 [M title page]
- Manual copyright 1994; on sale by early 1994 (dealer ads in Popular Photography Feb/Mar 1994, "Call" for price)
- Launch MSRP not found; 1995 dealer ads say $999.95 / $999 (Stereo Review 1995); $759.95 NTSC / $849.95 PAL by 1998 (Video Magazine)
- "The MX-1 virtually created the affordable digital video mixer market in 1994" (Focus Enhancements SVP, 2004) [FE]; "a product for the mass audience" [VM]
- Replaced by the MXPro (1997; 501 transitions vs the MX-1's 210; adds a joystick) [VM][FE]
- Videonics founded July 1986; IPO 15 Dec 1994 [SEC]; merged into Focus Enhancements 16 Jan 2001

## Inputs / outputs
- "4-input switcher, digital frame synchronizer and special effects mixer with dual time base corrector" [M p.76]
- 4 x S-Video + 4 x composite inputs; inputs 1-3 carry audio too, input 4 is video only [M p.76, rear-panel diagram]
- Outputs: MAIN and PREVIEW composite, S-Video MAIN, audio L/R, headphones [M p.76]
- PREVIEW shows all four inputs live as thumbnails plus the effect palette [M p.27, p.44]
- TBC "is always active - it cannot be disconnected or disabled" [M p.65]; dual TBC mode corrects two sources at once [M p.65]
- GPI mini-jack: a pulse "causes the Mixer to behave exactly as if the PLAY button were pressed" [M p.71, p.76]
- Audio: mix, fade, audio-follows-video or separate [M p.76, p.37]

## Front panel
- Take Bar, PLAY (Auto-Take), SPEED, FREEZE, WIPE / FADE-DISSOLVE / FLIP / ZOOM-P-IN-P, INPUT F/X, BACK COLOR, BORDER COLOR, AUDIO/VIDEO, keypad 0-9 with OK and REVERSE, arrows, DISPLAY, DEMO, SETUP, LEARN, CHROMA KEY, COMPOSE, SHIFT; a CUT row and a NEXT row of A/B/C/D/COLOR [M inside cover]
- CUT buttons set the CURRENT source, ABCD the NEXT; they swap after each transition [M p.34]
- "The name 'T-bar' is short for 'Take-bar'"; it must start from either end [M p.34]
- 10 auto speeds, 0 slowest to 9 fastest [M p.33, p.76]
- 210 effects ("over 200"): dissolves, wipes, slides, compress/expand, fly-in, flips, zooms, luminance key; numbered 0-239 [M p.76, p.32, p.87-88]
- Effect 0 = Cut, 1 = Dissolve, 30 = H Wipe, 212 = Butterfly, 221 = Mosaic Zoom, 234 = Checkerboard, 237/238 = Luminance Key [M p.83, p.53]
- "simply press the digits and immediately PLAY to perform the effect" [M p.32]
- Input effects per source: B&W, B/W negative, colour negative, flip H/V, mosaic 0-3, strobe 0-7 (1 = "film look", 2 = "old movie"), paint 0-7, solarization [M p.45, p.76]
- FREEZE captures a field; SHIFT+FREEZE is stop-motion snapshot mode [M p.46]
- Chroma key with an on-screen cursor, "normally found only in high-end professional equipment" [M p.48-51]
- Two colour generators, 1440 colours [M p.76]; COLOR is itself a source [M p.40]
- PIP: movable, sizable, bordered; "Smooth motion of the PIP window is not possible" [M p.54-56]
- COMPOSE: "video painting system" [M p.3, p.57]
- LEARN stores up to 25 effects (50 steps), lost at power-off [M p.62-64]
- "The MX-1 is not an edit controller" [M p.4]; no serial or MIDI

## Technical
- 13.5 MHz, 4:2:2, 8-bit, 4x subcarrier oversampling; S/N 56 dB; 5.0 MHz resolution; RS-170A time base [M p.76]
- NTSC; PAL version available [M p.76]
- 300 x 165 x 99 mm, 1.8 kg; 12 VAC adapter [M p.76]
- A VJ user reports about half a second of switch lag (audiofanzine review)

## Quotable
- "These mixers were quickly adopted by VJs as the core component of their performance setups." (Wikipedia, VJing, uncited) [WP]
- The manual's own nightclub example: a music video on A, "a camera aimed at the dance floor" on B [M p.44]
- A 1995 Club Nightflight plan put an MX-1 and two monitors in the DJ booth (Lighting Dimensions 1996); a 1999 New York club list includes one (Entertainment Design 1999)
- Videonics' 1994 tapes survive: "Quick Start Video" https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0pzoeThLjyo, "A Perfect Mix" https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s6W3y7W58d4
