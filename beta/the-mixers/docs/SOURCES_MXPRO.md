# Videonics MXPro: sourced facts

Gathered 2026-10-23 for the MXPro deck. Every caption on that deck comes from a line here.

Sources: [M] MXProDV User Guide MANL-0771-01 (the MXPro manual with the FireWire chapter; its Appendix E says it "helps Videonics MXPro and MX-1 users transition") https://medienwissenschaft.uni-bayreuth.de/wp-content/uploads/assets/PDFs_intern_MFu/Videonics-MX-ProDVUserManual.pdf · [VM1] Videomaker, Aug 1998 https://www.videomaker.com/article/c5/3497-mx-1-video-mixer-is-a-product-for-the-mass-audience/ · [VM2] Videomaker, June 1999 https://www.videomaker.com/article/7529-digital-video-mixer-review-videonics-mxpro/ · [VM3] Videomaker MXProDV review https://www.videomaker.com/article/7568-review-videonics-mxprodv-digital-video-mixer/ · [VM4] Videomaker news Aug 1999 https://www.videomaker.com/videonews/1999/08/videonics-ships-mxprodv/ · [FE] Focus Enhancements 2004 release https://www.videomaker.com/videonews/2004/08/focus-enhancements-releases-new-video-mixers/ · [WR] https://widescreenreview.com/news_detail.php?id=5476

## Identity
- Videonics, Inc., 1370 Dell Avenue, Campbell, CA 95008 [VM1][M p.3]
- Introduced 1997 ("further strengthened in 1997 with the introduction of MXPro") [FE]; MXPRO trademark filed 21 May 1997 (seen in a search snippet only)
- List price $1,799 [VM1][VM2][VM3]
- "the MXPro, Videonics upgrade for the MX-1 video mixer" [VM1]; "The MX-1 virtually created the affordable digital video mixer market in 1994" [FE]
- MX-1 had 210 transitions; MXPro has 501 [VM1]
- Model number MX-3000 [M p.3]
- MXProDV shipped Aug 1999 at $2,495: two FireWire inputs and one FireWire output added to the four analog inputs; "the first live mixer combining FireWire/i.Link with a full set of analog inputs and outputs" [VM4][VM3]
- Focus Enhancements completed the Videonics merger 16 Jan 2001 [WR]; successor MX-4 announced 2004 at $2,195 [FE]

## Inputs / outputs
- 4 x S-Video + 4 x composite inputs, any four at once [VM1][M App.D]
- 4 stereo audio inputs; it mixes audio too (Mix, Fade, Background) [M App.D][M ch.4]
- Outputs: 2 x composite + 2 x S-Video MAIN, 1 x composite PREVIEW [M App.D][VM1]
- Audio out: 2 stereo pairs + headphones [M App.D]
- GPI input on a mini-jack: a pulse acts as PLAY [M App.D][M p.152]
- Preview shows CURRENT, NEXT, both side by side with the transitions menu, or all four inputs as a quad [VM1]
- "Four Input Synchronized Switcher": frame synchronizer so switching never disrupts [M p.4]; TBC always on at the output [M App.B]
- ROUTE re-maps any rear jack to channels A-D [M p.92]

## Front panel
- Not A/B buses: a CURRENT row and a NEXT row of source buttons A, B, C, D plus COLOR; the two swap after each transition [M ch.4]
- T-bar ("Take Bar"): runs transitions by hand, also PIP size and audio levels [M ch.4]; reviewers found it slack ("still difficult to use") [VM2], about a second of lag [VM3]
- 501 transitions [M App.D][VM1]; "over 500 effects" [M p.4]; list numbers run to 706 [M App.A]
- Five category buttons: USER, BASIC, EDGES, TRAILING, SHAPES [M ch.4][VM1]
- Shapes: circle, star, square, heart, diamond, keyhole, binocular, anti-aliased [VM1]
- Numeric keypad 0-9 enters a transition by number and sets speed [M ch.4][VM2]
- PLAY performs the transition; press it repeatedly to wipe back and forth [M ch.2, ch.4]
- SPEED 0 (slowest) to 9 (fastest); REVERSE flips direction [M ch.2, ch.4]
- CHROMAKEY button; joystick positions the key cursor [M p.4, p.125]; luma key not found
- BG COLOR, BORDER COLOR, BORDER STYLE; two colour generators, "Millions of Colors" [M App.D]; default palette of ten colours [VM1]
- FREEZE: up to two sources frozen, field or frame [M p.97, p.90]
- Input effects: STROBE 0-9 (off during transitions), MOSAIC, POSTERIZE, B&W, COLOR NEG, FLIP, COLOR CORRECT [M p.76-81][VM1]
- COMPOSE: "a video painting system", up to 16 elements on one screen [M p.5, p.107]
- Multi-PIP: up to 16 images, 9 tile layouts; the joystick is new since the MX-1 [M p.4, p.107][VM1]
- LEARN: 8 environments survive power-off; scripts of up to 250 steps do not [M p.130-133]
- SHIFT+DEMO loops a self-demo [M p.86]; built-in colour bars [M p.148]

## Technical
- NTSC; PAL version available [M App.D]
- 13.5 MHz, 4:2:2, 10-bit Y/C and 8-bit composite A/D; S/N > 60 dB Y/C; 480 TV lines; CCIR-601 sampling [M App.D][VM1]
- 422 x 241 x 76 mm, 1.8 kg; external PSU [M App.D]; "16.6 x 9.5 x 3 in, 4 lb" [VM1]

## Quotable
- "so easy that nearly anyone can pick it up and use it proficiently within an hour" [VM2]
- The manual suggests it for security-camera quad monitoring: "simply press the CUT button for that camera" [M p.152]
