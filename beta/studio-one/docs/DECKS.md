# Studio One - deck sheets

Everyone forgets. The record doesn't.


## Deck 1 - Studio One

CBS Studio One, fourth floor, 485 Madison Avenue, 8:00 pm, Sunday 30 October 1938: a windowless, double-height room of microphones, music stands and a foley table that looks like a machine shop. Silver-gelatin newsreel light - charcoal blacks, ivory highlights, soft halation round the practical lamps - and one amber, the glow of the board behind the glass. The RCA 44-BX is the hero object, a chrome diamond that hears front and back and nothing at its sides. Typed copy in Courier, the network's serif for the opening.

## Deck 2 - Grovers Mill

The accounts, exactly as the soundtrack gives them: the observatory, the Wilmuth farm, the hum, the cap turning, the feelers in bad light, the heat ray from the mirror, the dead line, forty people. Every scene here is inside a reconstruction, so the optical soundtrack strip runs down the left edge. Night is charcoal-to-grey; the light is car headlights and one beam. The crowd is silhouettes; Phillips is never shown dead.

## Deck 3 - The Visitors

The r04 alternate history: the invasion is real, and two visitors with FCC credentials make the broadcasters call it theatre. They never speak, never hurry, never touch first; they knock while holding the keys. A black sedan at the curb, a brass revolving door, a card read with moving lips, a latch, a bare ridged hand on the board, and the red pencil. Red exists only on pencil marks: three clips in the whole composition.

## Deck 5 - Morning After

9:00 pm and after: the heavy doors burst open on dark uniforms, the switchboard becomes a vast sea of light, flashbulbs at the morning press conference, and the front pages. Then the correction the century took to make: about two percent of the audience was listening, and the panic was printed, not broadcast. It ends on the disc in the rehearsal rack with a small circle on its sleeve, and next week's script already red-pencilled - the one other place red is allowed.
